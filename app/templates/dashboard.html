OBSERVED · LOCAL REGISTER · DEFINITION {{ observed.definition_version }}

Measured from saved records

These counts come from the local SQLite register. Assessment volume is how many assessments are saved. Persisted dispositions are the human decisions saved on those assessments (approve, approve with conditions, escalate, or reject). A public /try/ run that is not saved is in neither number, and it is not a register disposition. Self-reported human handling time is optional minutes entered on a decision. Self-reported rework time is a separate optional number for time spent redoing work after a first pass. Self-reported manual touches are a separate optional count of human touchpoints on that assessment. Self-reported handoffs are a separate optional count of times the assessment was handed between people or queues before disposition. Each is shown when at least one decision has that number. Monthly assessment rate is a separate count of current decisions whose timestamp falls in the 30 days ending at the latest readable decision. It is shown only when those timestamps cover at least 30 days. A shorter history says Not measured. Authenticated effort is a separate optional number of minutes a signed-in reviewer entered on a disposition. It is shown when at least one decision has that number. A public /try/ save does not collect it. It is not a session timer. Cash savings are a separate optional number of whole dollars a signed-in reviewer entered when a spending reduction was known. The card shows the median when at least one decision has that number, and Not measured when none do. A public /try/ save does not collect them. They are not a wage times the other figures. Avoided-incident value is a separate modeled-assumption card. It says Not measured while incident_cost and avoided_incident_count are null. It is not an observed register figure, not cash savings, and not the labor-capacity panel. None of these figures is the modeled panel below. An empty register shows zeros for the counts that are counts. Definitions, rounding, and unmeasured quantities.
